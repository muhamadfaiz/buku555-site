Buku 555 — Support

Updated 17 November 2026

Buku 555 is an expense tracker for iPhone that fills itself in: card payments are caught automatically and receipt photos read themselves, so you mostly just confirm. Your spending stays on your own iPhone.

How do card payments get added on their own?

When you pay with a card in Apple Wallet, a Shortcuts personal automation you set up once sends that payment to Buku 555, which files it as a draft in the Review tab. Confirm it, edit it, or discard it. A draft does not count toward your totals until you confirm it.

Setup takes about a minute. Open the in-app guide — you can reach it from either of these places:

Settings → Automatic capture → Wallet Shortcut guide, or
Review tab → How to set up

In short, the guide walks you through this in the Shortcuts app:

  1. Open Shortcuts and go to the Automation tab.
  2. Add an automation, and under Apps choose “Wallet – When I tap a Wallet Card or Pass”.
  3. Tick the card(s) you pay with.
  4. Add the action Log card tap (Buku 555).
  5. Optional: map Merchant, Amount and Card to the automation’s input so they fill in by themselves. If you skip this, each tap still makes a draft you can fill in.
  6. Turn off Show When Run, then finish.

Test it without paying. Because the automation only fires from a real tap, the guide has a “Test it” button. Tap it and the app creates a test draft through the exact same path a real tap uses, so you can see how Review works even if you have nothing to buy right now.

To be told when a new draft is waiting, keep “Notify me on a new tap” on in Settings (Automatic capture). These are local notifications on your phone.

Why isn’t my Touch ’n Go eWallet or DuitNow QR payment caught?

By design. The automation can only see transactions Apple Pay already recorded as a Wallet card tap. An e-wallet balance and a QR or DuitNow transfer are not card taps, so nothing on the phone can catch them automatically. For those, add the expense by hand (the “+” button) or scan the receipt.

Do I tap the plastic card or my phone?

Pay with Apple Pay on the iPhone, not the physical plastic card. The automation watches the phone’s Wallet transaction. A tap-to-pay with the plastic card in a terminal is not seen by the app.

How does receipt reading work?

Tap Scan and point the camera at a receipt. The app crops it and reads the shop, date, total and line items.

  • On this iPhone (the default): reading happens on the device with Apple’s own text recognition. It is free, private, and works offline — nothing leaves your phone for this to work.
  • Google AI (optional): reads a few more receipts correctly, but only if you turn it on yourself in Settings → Receipt reading and paste your own Google AI key (a free key from aistudio.google.com). When it is on, each receipt photo you scan is sent to Google to be read; your key is kept in this iPhone’s Keychain and never sent to us. If there is no key or Google cannot be reached, the app simply reads the receipt on the phone instead.
  • Off: the photo is kept with the expense and you type the details.

How do I export, back up, or restore?

All of this is in Settings → Data:

  • Export CSV — a spreadsheet-friendly file of your confirmed expenses.
  • Back up to a file — a full backup you can import again later.
  • Import expenses — restores from a backup file, or brings in expenses from the old Buku 555 (a JSON file). Importing the same file again adds nothing, so it is safe to repeat.

Both export options open the normal iOS share sheet, so you choose where the file goes (Files, email, wherever). The CSV export covers confirmed expenses; drafts still in Review are not exported.

Where is my data stored?

Only on this iPhone. Your expenses, receipt photos, tags and settings live in the app’s own on-device store. We run no server and never receive your data; it is not uploaded anywhere by us. The app works offline and there is no account and no sign-in. As with any app, your data is included only in your own iPhone and iCloud device backups, which Apple manages and you control.

How do I delete everything?

To remove a single expense, swipe it in Home or delete it from the edit screen. To clear the lot at once, delete the app from your iPhone — that removes the app and everything stored on the device with it. If you want to keep the data first, back it up to a file (Settings → Data) before deleting the app.
